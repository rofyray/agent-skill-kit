# Discovery and Content

## Inspect before interviewing

Read what is available: brief, current pages, project conventions, design system, approved copy, assets, reference sites, and prior decisions. State when a link or image could not be inspected. Do not invent its contents.

Resolve only consequential gaps:

- Subject and audience: who this is for and what they need to understand.
- Visitor goal: buy, book, learn, compare, contact, experience, or navigate onward.
- Scope: new page, multi-page site, existing-site revision, plan, or review.
- Identity: brand voice, visual constraints, references, and decisions already accepted.
- Resources: supplied imagery, product screenshots, clips, sound demonstrations, fonts, and implementation environment.
- Constraints: time, media spending, performance expectations, required interactions, and review checkpoints.

Offer a small set of meaningful choices when the direction is open. Use ordinary prose if the host has no choice UI. A complete brief needs no ritual questionnaire.

## Preserve the subject's truth

For a real business, use its real name, verified offerings, and approved assets. No usable photos is a media gap, not permission to invent a brand, team, location, or product. Label conceptual imagery when it could be mistaken for evidence of the actual product, property, or service. Do not imply generated interiors or product mechanisms are accurate without supporting material.

For an explicitly fictional concept, invent consistently and make the demo status clear where a visitor could otherwise mistake it for a real offer. Use labeled sample proof rather than fabricated customer endorsements.

Screenshots and exact product UI should remain crisp and faithful. Use them directly or animate controlled layers; do not force them through generative video that changes labels or controls. Preserve a person's identity and the authorized use of supplied likeness assets. Do not reinterpret a supplied image as permission for a new endorsement.

## Research selectively

When research would improve unfamiliar audience language or objection handling, inspect a small set of relevant public sources or supplied customer evidence. Record source, date where relevant, recurring concern, and the design implication. Separate a customer's phrase from a verified claim about this business.

If browsing is unavailable, use supplied facts and mark audience hypotheses as assumptions. Do not claim research occurred. Generic category research cannot substantiate this company's testimonials, awards, results, integrations, or prices.

Translate findings into concise original copy. Use exact quotations only when their source and use are appropriate. A personal portfolio may open with the maker's point of view; a comparison page may lead with a decision aid. Choose the opening for the visitor and subject instead of imposing a pain-hook formula.

## Structure a complete site

Give each section a job: introduce, demonstrate, explain, substantiate, address an objection, or offer a next action. Preserve necessary navigation, legal content supplied by the user, contact information, and secondary user tasks. A primary action creates clarity but does not justify deleting everything else.

Use proof that exists: actual work, accurate screenshots, usable demos, documented results, or approved quotations. If proof is absent, show the product or process honestly and identify what is missing. Do not manufacture ratings, client logos, trust badges, counters, or scarcity.

Choose section composition according to its content. Vary rhythm where useful while keeping repeated components consistent when they represent equivalent information. Do not decorate every section to satisfy a motion quota.

## Write copy deliberately

Record important strings and their approval status in the design package. Use specific nouns, concrete outcomes, and the brand's register. Remove vague filler and accidental stock phrasing by reading the rendered copy in context. A mechanical word blacklist must not corrupt a real product name, quoted statement, technical term, or user-approved line.

Check the entire experience: navigation, hero, lower sections, labels, errors, empty states, and confirmations. Do not promise what an interaction does not perform.

For forms, identify the actual destination and existing integration. Preserve a working backend when present. A demo can use a clearly labeled non-submitting form; it must not claim a message was sent. A mail link opens an email draft, not a delivery confirmation. A real submission flow needs honest pending, success, and error behavior, with success tied to the integration's response. If the endpoint is unavailable, mark the integration incomplete instead of displaying a false success.

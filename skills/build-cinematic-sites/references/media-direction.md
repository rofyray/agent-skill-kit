# Media Direction and Optional Companions

## Choose a treatment for the page

Decide what the visual must communicate before choosing its generator. A supplied photograph with a controlled reveal may serve the brief better than a new film. Use generated imagery for atmosphere or concepts it can represent faithfully; use controlled graphics for exact product details, type, UI, geometry, and timing. Combine them when each has a clear job.

| Treatment | Good fit | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static or lightly animated imagery | Quiet identity, limited assets, short visitor task | Empty spectacle or missing content hidden by the hero |
| Single scroll-driven shot | A readable approach, reveal, pour, assembly, or material study | Cropping, seeking cost, weak ending, text competing with action |
| Chained journey | A justified sequence longer than the selected tool supports | Identity changes, motion discontinuity, payload, excessive scroll length |
| Controlled graphics or hybrid | Exact UI, type, diagrams, product states, or interactive explanation | Unnecessary complexity and inaccessible information inside pixels |

These are implementation treatments, not extra user modes or mandatory scope tiers. A choreographed page can use any of them.

## Compose before generating

Describe the subject's recognizable details, the intended reading regions, crop-safe margins, and the beginning and ending compositions. Test the ending with the actual navigation and final copy over it. A full-bleed surface may crop more reliably than a tightly framed object, but use it only when it serves the story.

For a scroll sequence, make forward and reverse movement understandable. A vertical direction can reinforce scrolling, but an orbit, sideways reveal, or controlled transformation can also work. Preserve ordinary page scrolling and avoid trapping the visitor in the sequence.

For continuous footage, define camera movement separately from subject movement. Keep identity, scale, lighting, and travel direction coherent. Allow natural movement where it helps the scene; do not contradict a required still hold. Describe boundary transitions physically when realism calls for it, without adding lens effects to every shot.

Reserve negative space as a calm part of the scene, such as a receding wall or soft background, instead of asking for blank black panels. Protect exact brand marks and supplied product appearance. For a clean generated plate, exclude unintended lettering and marks while preserving any specifically authorized label or logo. Do not combine a blanket no-logo instruction with a requirement to show the supplied logo.

## Compile a usable prompt package

Maintain the full creative specification, then map it to inputs the selected tool actually supports. A useful start-frame brief includes subject identity, composition, first state, light/material treatment, safe text regions, aspect, and reference roles. A motion brief includes the entering state, primary action, camera path, continuity, transitions, exit state, and permitted deviations.

Keep exact web copy out of generated scenery unless it is deliberately part of the object. State which elements will be overlaid or built directly in the page. Do not force photorealism, silence, six-second duration, or a fixed resolution on every request. Decorative scroll footage normally has no audio; user-controlled proof media may need its original sound and accessible alternatives.

If a provider offers presets, assess whether they preserve the approved treatment. Decline a mismatched preset, not every preset automatically. Model prompts are instructions to attempt an outcome, not proof that it occurred.

## Chain and inspect intentionally

Plan seams at a useful occlusion, blur, movement, or transition. Only the final segment needs a stable resting ending unless the design calls for intermediate holds. Texture, fine geometry, and lighting may change even when the next clip begins from the previous image; a shared frame does not guarantee seamless motion.

Inspect each segment before deriving its continuation. Extract the actual chosen last frame at full quality, register it through the current tool's supported input path, and carry forward the subject's state, motion, lighting, and composition. If the chosen model does not support this input, propose a controlled join or a different supported treatment before claiming continuity.

Review seams in forward and reverse scrubbing as well as ordinary playback. A crossfade may soften a seam but can create double silhouettes or apparent reversals; inspect it and remap the page after any duration change. Re-encode source clips consistently only after normalizing dimensions, cadence, aspect, timestamps, and color treatment as needed.

Inspect the whole artifact where playback is available, plus opening, high-risk, caption, seam, and ending frames. Check identity, geometry, unintended text, crop safety, readability, and the final state. Still samples alone cannot prove temporal quality. Preserve useful generated improvements only when they fit the brief and approved constraints.

Before another paid attempt, identify the failure's layer: prompt, source image, generation limit, edit, crop, page layout, or interaction. Repair the cheapest appropriate layer. Stop at the authorized limit; repeated failure should trigger a treatment review rather than indefinite rerolls.

## Optional skill discovery

Check the host's available skill catalog or accessible installed files. Mentioning a skill name is not evidence it is installed. When useful, read its actual entrypoint and use only the relevant workflow. This is optional skill composition, not an instruction to spawn agents or delegate work.

| Companion | Use when | Ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `design-beyond-defaults` | The brief needs stronger art direction, meaningful concept alternatives, interface critique, or a finishing pass. | It develops or evaluates the interface direction; this skill maintains content, site integration, and verification. |
| `ai-motion-graphics-direction` | Product assembly, architectural transformation, kinetic type, overlays, diagrams, or mixed-media production benefits from specialized direction. | It directs the requested media or motion specification; this skill owns the web experience and accessible content. |

Keep one shared brief and preserve locked requirements. Do not require both companions, replay their full intake, duplicate approval gates, or copy their libraries into this package. A new style request should change composition and motion where appropriate, not only recolor an old treatment.

If a useful companion is absent, give the relevant repository link:

Do not install it automatically or claim the skill also connects a generator. Continue independent site work; offer the self-contained route if the user does not want the companion. When the user explicitly requires it, prepare the handoff and state what awaits its availability.

## Shared handoff and return

Pass the relevant site package, approved copy and reference IDs, locked identity/facts, style direction, asset purpose, dimensions/crops, safe text regions, start/end states, intended seams, and budget/attempt limits. State whether the deliverable is a linear asset, editable graphic, or scroll-controlled specification.

For motion direction, state that page labels and actions remain semantic web content. Specify which graphics belong in the media and which will be implemented in the browser. Avoid asking the media skill to render the entire website as a video.

On return, record the actual artifact, settings, dependencies, inspection status, duration, crop, and unresolved gaps. Translate its timed beats into page progress and reading windows. Inspect reversibility and actual seams. Reassess asset size, static alternatives, and caption placement using the delivered footage, not the original storyboard alone.

For design critique, supply rendered evidence when available and return a prioritized set of changes against the same brief. Do not let a stylistic suggestion override required behavior or approved identity. Apply changes only within the user's requested mode.

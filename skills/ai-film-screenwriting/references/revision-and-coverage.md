# Revision and Coverage

Use this reference to diagnose, rewrite, compare, or evaluate an outline or screenplay. Separate analysis from mutation: coverage does not authorize a rewrite.

## Establish the review contract

Identify:

- The canonical version and the material actually available for review.
- The user's intended audience, genre, tone, and goals.
- Locked choices and known concerns.
- Whether the task is diagnosis, prioritized planning, targeted rewrite, full rewrite, comparison, or coverage only.
- The evidence granularity available: pages, scenes, outline steps, or excerpts.

Do not make feature-wide claims after reading only an excerpt. Scope the finding to the material inspected.

## Diagnose in passes

Use only the passes relevant to the request, but consider upstream causes before polishing symptoms.

### Story engine and structure

- Clarity and durability of the dramatic question.
- Protagonist or ensemble agency.
- Opposition, stakes, urgency, escalation, and reversals.
- Causal links between major movements.
- Distribution of setup, discovery, consequence, and payoff.
- Genre promise, tonal control, and ending pressure.
- Redundant, missing, or misplaced sequences.

### Character and relationship

- Wants, choices, contradictions, thresholds, and arc progression.
- Relationship changes caused by events.
- Consistency between established capability and later action.
- Supporting characters with independent dramatic functions.
- Distinct voice and information ownership.

### Scene function

- Pursuit, resistance, tactic, turn, and consequence.
- Entry and exit points.
- Repetition of information or dramatic work.
- Balance of orientation, atmosphere, conflict, and change.
- Transition pressure between adjacent scenes.

### Dialogue and exposition

- Subtext, strategy, rhythm, interruption, silence, and distinctness.
- Speeches or exchanges serving only the audience.
- Repeated explanation after the point is already legible.
- Parentheticals and action lines that over-direct the performance.

### Continuity and readability

- Timeline, location, knowledge, injuries, props, costume states, and relationship status.
- Setups without payoffs and payoffs without sufficient preparation.
- Formatting, cue consistency, action clarity, and accidental ambiguity.

## Write evidence-based coverage

Unless the user supplies another template, return:

1. Scope and version reviewed.
2. Logline or concise story statement.
3. Synopsis including the ending, when full coverage is requested.
4. Intended experience and what the material currently delivers.
5. Strengths supported by specific scenes, beats, or passages.
6. Concerns supported by specific evidence and their likely upstream causes.
7. Prioritized recommendations, ordered by leverage and dependency.
8. Questions or tradeoffs that require the writer's choice.

Use scores, verdicts, or commercial recommendations only when requested or required by the user's coverage format. Label subjective judgments and avoid pretending to predict market outcomes.

## Design the revision

Prioritize changes that repair multiple downstream problems. For each proposed change, state:

- The diagnosed problem and evidence.
- The desired dramatic effect.
- The earliest scene or story assumption that must change.
- The scenes, setups, payoffs, and continuity fields affected.
- Locked material that must survive.
- The test that will show the revision worked.

Rewrite the smallest coherent unit that can actually solve the problem. A structural change may require a sequence rather than a sentence; a line-level issue should not trigger an unnecessary redesign.

## Compare versions

When comparing drafts:

- Align corresponding scenes even when numbering changed.
- Identify additions, removals, relocations, and altered causal links.
- Test the requested objective against the actual pages.
- Note gains, regressions, and new continuity obligations.
- Distinguish intentional difference from accidental drift.

Do not assume the newer version is better. Preserve a concise change log and identify which draft becomes canonical only when the user chooses or the request clearly establishes it.

## Verify a rewrite

After revising:

- Re-read the changed unit in surrounding context.
- Confirm the target problem improved on the page.
- Check locked lines, events, voice, and format.
- Recheck every affected setup, payoff, timeline fact, prop, and relationship state.
- Update the continuity and revision records.
- Report any consequence that still needs a later scene change.

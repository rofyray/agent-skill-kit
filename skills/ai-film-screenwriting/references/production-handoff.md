# Production Handoff

Use this reference when the user wants screenplay material prepared for visual development, performance direction, video direction, budgeting, or another production workflow.

The handoff translates story intent into stable facts. It does not add provider tags, model syntax, lenses, camera moves, image prompts, physical acting prescriptions, or unsupported design details.

## Core screenplay package

Provide the smallest useful set:

- Project identity, version, format, genre, tone, audience, and logline.
- Current story bible and world rules.
- Character and relationship summaries with arc states.
- Approved outline or sequence map.
- Canonical screenplay or clearly identified excerpt.
- Continuity ledger and unresolved decisions.
- Rights, research, language, rating, or representation notes that materially affect production.

Mark every item as locked, provisional, or open when ambiguity could cause downstream drift.

## Scene intent sheet

For each scene being handed off, include:

- Scene identifier, heading, story position, and canonical version.
- Dramatic purpose and genre function.
- Characters present and required narrative props or environmental facts.
- State entering: knowledge, relationship, physical condition, resources, and pressure.
- Immediate pursuit, opposition, stakes, hidden information, and decisive turn.
- Required actions and exact dialogue that cannot change.
- State leaving and continuity passed to the next scene.
- Setups, payoffs, reveals, and ambiguity that must remain legible.
- Production constraints stated by the user.

Do not invent exact character counts for background people, geography, duration, shot design, or visual continuity unless the screenplay or user establishes them.

## Visual-development handoff

Export narrative requirements rather than final designs:

- Character age range or life stage when story-relevant, role, social context, and recognition anchors established by the script.
- Costume milestones, disguises, injuries, weathering, or state changes required by the story.
- Locations and props with narrative functions, periods, cultural constraints, transformations, and repeated use.
- World rules, palette implications explicitly stated by the user, and forbidden contradictions.
- Separate states that must not be collapsed into one reference, such as hidden versus revealed mechanisms or pre-injury versus post-injury appearance.

Leave aesthetic invention open unless it is already locked.

## Performance-direction handoff

Export playable dramatic facts:

- Scene objective, obstacle, stakes, relationship status, and power shifts.
- What each character knows, wants, hides, expects, and risks.
- Tactical changes implied by the written scene.
- Subtextual contradiction and the scene's decisive change.
- Exact dialogue, intended language, and any voice facts established by the project.

Do not prescribe blinking, gestures, breath patterns, facial movements, or line readings unless they are indispensable screenplay actions or explicitly requested.

## Video-direction handoff

Export production-ready story facts:

- Scene order, time, location, participating characters, required props, and continuity state.
- Canonical action and dialogue.
- Narrative geography established by the script.
- Required transition, reveal, concealment, threshold, or cause-and-effect relationship.
- Story-critical duration or simultaneity only when established.

Leave shot count, blocking, optics, camera, timing, lighting implementation, audio design, physics detail, and generation constraints to video direction.

## Handoff verification

- Every exported fact is supported by the canonical story material or labeled as a proposal.
- Locked dialogue and continuity are exact.
- Different character, costume, prop, and location states remain distinguishable.
- No downstream discipline is forced to reverse-engineer the scene's dramatic purpose.
- The packet is understandable without access to this skill or any other skill.

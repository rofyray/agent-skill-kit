# Voice and Dialogue Direction

Use this reference for voice identity, scene delivery, exact lines, accents, multilingual speech, voice tests, lip sync, and speaker ownership.

## Separate identity from delivery

Maintain two layers:

### Voice identity anchor

Stable properties that keep the character recognizable:

- Language and dialect or accent.
- Register, timbre, resonance, articulation, and vocal age range.
- Habitual pace, rhythm, vocabulary, sentence construction, and use of silence.
- Stable physiological or story conditions.
- Boundaries that distinguish the character from the rest of the cast.

### Scene delivery overlay

Variable properties caused by the current objective and body:

- Tactic and intended effect on the listener.
- Pace, volume, pitch movement, emphasis, interruption, overlap, and pause behavior.
- Breath support, exertion, pain, illness, crying, laughter, whispering, eating, running, restraint, or physical contact.
- Concealment, contradiction, status shift, and what changes mid-line.
- State carried into and out of the line.

Keep identity stable without pasting one unchanging emotional description into every scene. The same voice can hurry, fracture, flatten, soften, or become more controlled while remaining the same person.

## Preserve exact dialogue and ownership

- Quote final lines exactly, including case, punctuation, language, intentional fragments, and approved overlaps.
- Assign every spoken line to one speaker.
- Mark silence explicitly only when the tool or scene needs it.
- Do not give silent characters accidental vocalizations, repeated words, or lip movement.
- Distinguish dialogue, exertion, breath, nonverbal vocalization, and sound effects when the tool exposes separate fields.
- Preserve key words during overlap and confirm that lip sync follows the correct speaker.

Do not rewrite dialogue while directing it unless the user asks. If the line cannot fit the required duration intelligibly, report the conflict and offer timing or writing options without silently changing the text.

## Direct speech as action

Describe what the line is doing to the listener: reassuring, cornering, delaying, testing, seducing, humiliating, bargaining, protecting, provoking, or concealing. Let delivery change when the tactic changes.

Avoid generic requests such as emotional, intense, dramatic, or natural. Replace them with objective, physical state, relationship, and observable vocal behavior.

Use pauses only when the character assesses, decides, refuses, recovers, listens, or changes strategy. Silence without an event reads as delay rather than thought.

## Handle accent and language responsibly

Describe dialect through region, language history, rhythm, vowel or consonant tendencies when genuinely needed, and degree of strength. Avoid phonetic spelling that harms readability or turns identity into caricature.

For multilingual scenes, preserve the exact language of each line, code-switching points, pronunciation guidance supplied by qualified sources, and who understands each language. Do not invent translations or cultural claims when accuracy matters and verification is unavailable.

## Use voice references safely

Assign each audio reference a role such as identity, pronunciation, delivery, timing, or physical state. A delivery reference should not silently replace the canonical voice identity.

For cloning, imitation, or transformation of a real person's voice, confirm authorization and intended use before sending a reference to an external service. Do not imply consent, ownership, or clearance. Keep private recordings and permission evidence out of prompts and logs unless strictly required.

## Build a voice test

Choose lines that expose the project's real risks:

- Neutral identity and ordinary speech.
- Pressure without shouting.
- Fast or overlapping dialogue while preserving intelligibility.
- Low-volume or intimate speech.
- Exertion, injury, laughter, crying, whisper, or another important physical condition.
- Language, accent, name, or terminology that needs pronunciation verification.

Use a small representative set rather than generating every scene. Compare identity stability, intelligibility, timing, tactic, breath, artifacts, and compatibility with lip sync.

## Verify audio and lip sync

Inspect the returned asset when possible:

- Voice identity remains consistent with the approved anchor.
- The line is exact and belongs to the correct speaker.
- Delivery pursues the intended objective rather than displaying a generic emotion.
- Breath and vocal effort match visible or stated physical action.
- Pauses, overlaps, emphasis, and pace are intentional and intelligible.
- Pronunciation, language, and accent meet the approved target.
- Lip movement begins, ends, and articulates with the correct audio.
- No unrequested speech, background voice, clone drift, or audio artifact is present.

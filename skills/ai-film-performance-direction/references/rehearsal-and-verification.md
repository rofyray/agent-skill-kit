# Rehearsal, Verification, and Repair

Use this reference to design controlled takes, inspect written or generated performance, diagnose failure, make focused corrections, and manage retries.

## Rehearse a hypothesis

Each alternative take should test a dramatic choice, not a generic emotion setting. Hold identity, dialogue, blocking, duration, and unrelated production choices constant while changing one meaningful variable:

- Tactic.
- Degree or method of concealment.
- Timing of recognition or decision.
- Relationship distance or touch.
- Physical business and its interruption.
- Tempo, stillness, breath, or vocal pressure.
- Which of two truthful impulses wins at the end of the beat.

State the predicted effect and comparison criterion before generating. Use the smallest number of takes that can answer the question.

## Inspect the actual performance

Evaluate the returned text, audio, animation, or video rather than the prompt alone:

1. Objective and tactic are legible through behavior.
2. Listening and assessment occur at truthful moments.
3. Beat changes have triggers and visible or audible consequences.
4. Body, gaze, breath, business, voice, and physical conditions agree.
5. Dialogue and nonverbal sound belong to the correct speaker.
6. Relationship, status, distance, and contact remain coherent.
7. Voice identity, character core, and state continuity hold.
8. Performance density fits duration, framing, and focus.
9. No tool artifacts, frozen features, accidental synchronization, or unrequested behavior undermine the take.

If the host cannot play or inspect the media, state that verification is limited.

## Diagnose failure patterns

### Indicated emotion

Symptom: mugging, generic expressive face, or behavior that illustrates an emotion label.

Repair: restore the objective and obstacle, give the character a tactic or task, and remove redundant facial instructions.

### Waiting for a cue

Symptom: a character is blank while the partner speaks, then activates only on their own line.

Repair: specify what they understand, resist, anticipate, or decide during the partner's action.

### Monotactic performance

Symptom: one tempo, volume, posture, or strategy across the scene.

Repair: identify the trigger where the tactic fails and define one consequential adjustment.

### Unearned change

Symptom: tears, rage, violence, affection, or collapse appears without sufficient cause or resistance.

Repair: restore the sequence of restraint, pressure, trigger, decision, and aftermath at a scale the shot can support.

### Decorative behavior

Symptom: constant tics, eye movement, gestures, business, or pauses with no motivation.

Repair: remove behavior that lacks a trigger or tactical function. Preserve chosen stillness when it is active.

### Physical contradiction

Symptom: breath, voice, balance, contact, pain, or effort ignores the action and environment.

Repair: synchronize vocal and body consequences with the actual physical state.

### Ensemble cloning

Symptom: characters react identically, move together unintentionally, or share the same performance rhythm.

Repair: restore individual attention, knowledge, objectives, and recognition timing.

### State reset

Symptom: the next line, shot, or scene loses the prior event's physical or relationship consequence.

Repair: carry forward the specific residue and define what event changes it.

### Voice or ownership drift

Symptom: timbre, accent, language, line, speaker, or lip sync changes incorrectly.

Repair: reassert the voice identity anchor, exact dialogue, speaker assignment, and tool-specific synchronization fields.

## Make a focused correction

Name the observable failure, its trigger or missing cause, the target behavior, and the invariants to preserve. Change one failure class per correction when possible. Return to canonical character, voice, or scene references when iterative generations have drifted.

Do not solve weak acting by adding more simultaneous instructions. Simplify to the decisive objective, listening event, tactic change, and physical consequence.

## Control cost and retries

Track take ID, provider, model, version, references, settings, tested variable, result, and accepted or rejected status. Stop when:

- The take answers the rehearsal question or passes the required checks.
- The user-defined time or generation budget is exhausted.
- Another paid generation needs approval.
- Repeated takes show no measurable improvement.
- The issue requires a different duration, shot, source reference, voice anchor, blocking choice, or tool capability.

Do not impose a universal take count or performance score. Use criteria tied to the scene's dramatic purpose.

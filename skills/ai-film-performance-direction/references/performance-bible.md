# Character Performance Bible

Use this reference to define a recurring character's playable baseline, relationship behavior, and continuity anchors. The bible is a source of truth to adapt, not a paragraph that must be pasted unchanged into every scene.

## Start from dramatic function

Ground the performance in supplied story facts:

- Long-range pursuit, immediate needs, obligations, fears, contradictions, secrets, and thresholds.
- What the character believes about themself, the world, and each important relationship.
- Status the character claims, status others grant, and what destabilizes either one.
- Profession, culture, history, injuries, habits, environment, and physical conditions that visibly affect behavior.
- Arc states and cumulative changes across the story.

Do not invent trauma, diagnosis, addiction, accent, disability, or identity details merely to make a profile seem specific.

## Define the playable core

Use fields that change performance decisions:

- **Pursuit pattern**: how the character typically gets what they want.
- **Attention**: what they notice first, monitor, ignore, or avoid.
- **Listening**: how quickly they absorb information and how thought becomes visible before speech.
- **Physical baseline**: center, posture, weight, tempo, stillness, gait, gesture scale, breath, and use of space.
- **Social mask**: the behavior used to manage how others see them.
- **Pressure response**: what tightens, speeds, slows, fragments, freezes, leaks, or becomes more controlled.
- **Concealment behavior**: what the character does when hiding information or vulnerability.
- **Triggers and tells**: specific behaviors tied to specific conditions, only when character-relevant.
- **Recovery**: how long strong events remain in breath, body, attention, and speech.
- **Contradiction**: two truthful impulses that can coexist rather than one clean emotional label.
- **Limits**: clichés, gestures, rhythms, or choices that would contradict the established character.

Describe observable tendencies, not a mechanical loop. A tic without a trigger becomes decoration and should not be repeated in every scene.

## Map relationship-specific behavior

The same character behaves differently with different people. For each important relationship, record:

- What the character wants from that person and fears from them.
- Claimed and actual status.
- Comfortable distance, touch, eye contact, interruption, silence, and conversational rhythm.
- Topics, objects, or behaviors that shift the mask.
- What the character permits, conceals, performs, or cannot sustain with this person.
- How the relationship changes across the story.

Avoid treating proxemics or status signals as universal codes. Closing distance can mean intimacy, aggression, urgency, protection, or practical necessity depending on context.

## Keep performance and appearance separate

Use visual references to understand body proportions, injury, costume restrictions, props, and physical state. Do not copy wardrobe, lighting, camera, color, or image-prompt language into the performance bible unless it changes movement or vocal production.

An armored costume may restrict range of motion. A corset may alter breath. Wet shoes may change gait. Those are performance facts. The costume's color and rendering style are not.

## Define voice identity without freezing delivery

Record stable vocal anchors:

- Apparent age range when relevant to the fictional voice.
- Language, dialect or accent, register, timbre, resonance, articulation, and habitual pace.
- Speech construction, vocabulary, rhythm, use of silence, and recurring verbal strategy.
- Physiological or story conditions that remain stable.
- Boundaries that prevent drift into another character's voice.

Keep scene delivery separate. Volume, pace, breath, breaks, pitch movement, fluency, and silence may change with tactic and physical state while the identity remains recognizable.

## Version the bible

For a substantial project, record:

- Character and canonical version.
- Evidence sources and approved references.
- Stable core and relationship variants.
- Voice identity anchor and authorization status for real references.
- Arc or degradation state by sequence when needed.
- Approved performance tests and known failure patterns.
- Open decisions and superseded choices.

Update the bible only when the project's character understanding changes. A scene-specific adaptation belongs in its scene packet, not in the permanent core.

## Verify the profile

- Every instruction can be played or observed.
- The character has methods, contradictions, and limits rather than a list of emotions.
- Behavior changes by relationship and pressure.
- Physical and vocal choices agree with established biography and conditions.
- The profile leaves room for scene-specific listening and discovery.
- No camera, grade, image style, provider syntax, or unsupported diagnosis leaked into the profile.

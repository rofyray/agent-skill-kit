# Scene Performance Direction

Use this reference to adapt character bibles to a specific scene, shot, sequence, or line without rewriting the screenplay or taking over cinematography.

## Establish the scene engine

For every active character, identify:

- State entering the scene: knowledge, relationship, physical condition, recent event, and unresolved pressure.
- Immediate objective expressed as an action toward a person or concrete obstacle.
- Opposition, internal restraint, and cost of failure.
- Public tactic, private tactic, and what the character is hiding from others or themself.
- What event can make the current tactic fail or become unnecessary.
- State leaving the scene and what must carry forward.

The objective should be playable, such as make her stay, force him to admit it, buy time, regain control, or protect the child. Avoid objectives such as be angry, look scared, or act suspicious.

## Design beats from triggers

A beat changes when new information arrives, a tactic fails, power shifts, a decision lands, an action is interrupted, or the objective changes. For each visible beat, record:

- Trigger.
- Current tactic.
- Listening or assessment event.
- Observable adjustment in attention, breath, posture, business, distance, timing, or delivery.
- Consequence passed to the next beat.

Scale the map to duration and shot size. A very short close shot may contain one assessment and one decisive adjustment. A long ensemble take may support several tactics and reactions. Do not force a fixed count or a visible change every second.

## Adapt the stable core

Preserve the character's pursuit pattern, physical logic, relationship behavior, voice identity, attention, and pressure responses. Adapt how those tendencies can appear in the current circumstances.

If a habitual behavior is physically impossible, translate its function rather than deleting the character logic. A pacing character trapped in a seat may redirect the same pressure into foot tension, object handling, posture shifts, or compressed speech. Do not insert a signature tic merely to prove continuity.

## Direct listening and thought

Listening is active behavior, not an empty face until a cue:

- Let a character understand, resist, misread, or anticipate while the partner is speaking.
- Give important information an assessment event proportional to its impact and the available time.
- Let gaze follow thought and task. Avoid continuous eye choreography that turns attention into noise.
- Use interruption, overlap, delayed answer, topic shift, business, or silence when they serve a tactic.
- Let the partner's behavior alter tempo, volume, distance, posture, or strategy when truthful.

A pause needs an event inside it. Remove pauses that exist only to signal importance.

## Give the body a task

Use business when it creates truth, pressure, rhythm, or subtext. Define:

- The practical task and why the character is doing it now.
- How competence, distraction, concealment, urgency, or relationship affects the task.
- What interruption or stopping point becomes dramatically meaningful.
- Physical consequences of exertion, injury, cold, heat, intoxication, costume restriction, or contact.

Do not give every character constant hand activity. Chosen stillness can be active when attention and intention remain alive.

## Fit the shot without directing it

Use known duration, framing, and blocking as constraints:

- Reduce gesture amplitude and competing behaviors when a tight frame makes small thought changes legible.
- Use broader body and spatial choices when the full body or group relationship is visible.
- Prioritize one readable performance event when time is short.
- Keep movement within established geography and physical possibility.

Do not choose lenses, camera moves, edit points, or lighting. Flag when the supplied shot prevents a required story reaction from being visible, but leave the production solution open.

## Compile the scene performance block

Include only the needed fields:

- Character and active reference tag when the pipeline uses one.
- Entering state, objective, obstacle, stakes, and hidden information.
- Physical baseline adapted to the exact position and condition.
- Business, attention, listening, relationship distance, and contact.
- Triggered beat or tactic changes in chronological order.
- Dialogue delivery and silence, with voice identity referenced separately when supported.
- Leaving state and continuity trail.
- Performance exclusions that prevent known failure modes.

For a video model, express behavior as short observable states and triggered changes that fit the tool's timing syntax. Do not convert the full scene into abstract psychology or a long chain of transitions the model cannot resolve.

## Verify scene direction

- Every character acts on current knowledge rather than the scene's future result.
- Every beat has a trigger and consequence.
- The direction creates listening, not cue waiting.
- Behavior, dialogue, body, and physical conditions agree.
- The amount of direction fits duration, shot size, and model capability.
- The final state can carry into the next shot or scene.

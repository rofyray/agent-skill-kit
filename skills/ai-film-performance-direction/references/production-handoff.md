# Performance Production Handoff

Use this reference when organizing character performance for screenwriting, visual development, voice production, animation, video direction, editing, or another production stage.

The handoff must separate stable character identity from scene-specific direction and remain understandable without this skill or any other skill.

## Character performance package

Include the relevant subset:

- Character name, canonical version, dramatic role, and current arc state.
- Pursuit pattern, attention, listening, physical baseline, social mask, pressure response, concealment, recovery, contradictions, and limits.
- Relationship-specific behavior and status changes.
- Voice identity anchor and authorization status for real references.
- Physical conditions, injuries, costume restrictions, or props that affect movement or voice.
- Approved tests, known failures, locked choices, and open decisions.

Do not include camera, grade, visual style, costume appearance, or provider syntax unless another field explicitly requires it.

## Scene performance packet

For each active character, record:

- Scene and shot identifiers, story position, duration or timing constraint, and version.
- Entering knowledge, relationship, physical state, and unresolved pressure.
- Objective, obstacle, stakes, tactic, hidden information, and subtextual contradiction.
- Business, attention, listening, distance, contact, and adapted physical baseline.
- Beat triggers, tactic changes, and observable consequences.
- Exact dialogue and speaker ownership.
- Scene delivery overlay linked to the stable voice identity.
- Leaving state and continuity trail.
- Locked, provisional, open, and unverified decisions.

Use only characters active in the scene or shot. Do not attach unrelated profiles that may confuse a generation model.

## Voice and reference map

Assign one role to every input:

- Character or face identity.
- Voice identity.
- Pronunciation or language.
- Delivery or physical state.
- Timing and lip sync.
- Body motion or performance reference.
- Blocking or scene interaction.

State what each input must not control when contamination is likely. Record consent or rights status without exposing private evidence.

## Screenwriting feedback

Report when performance analysis reveals a writing issue:

- Objective, obstacle, information ownership, or relationship state is contradictory.
- A line cannot fit the required duration intelligibly.
- A tactic change or emotional turn lacks a trigger on the page.
- Physical action prevents the written speech or behavior.

Do not rewrite dialogue or story without authorization.

## Visual-development handoff

Request only visual facts needed for performance: canonical identity, body proportions, injury, costume restriction, prop relationship, physical state, and relevant scene frame. Do not ask visual development to encode acting tics or every emotional state into a character sheet.

## Video and animation handoff

Provide:

- Active characters and exact performance references.
- Adapted scene blocks and beat order.
- Blocking or contact already established by production.
- Exact dialogue, speaker ownership, voice identity, and delivery overlay.
- Physical state, effort, reaction timing, and continuity trail.
- Known duration, lip-sync, or model-capability constraints.

Leave optics, camera movement, edit structure, lighting implementation, physics elaboration, style syntax, and final prompt assembly to video direction.

## Handoff verification

- Stable identity and scene delivery are clearly separated.
- Objectives, tactics, triggers, and consequences are playable.
- Dialogue, voice, reactions, and references belong to the correct characters.
- Physical and relationship state carries across adjacent material.
- Rights-sensitive references are not exposed unnecessarily.
- The consumer knows what remains unverified.

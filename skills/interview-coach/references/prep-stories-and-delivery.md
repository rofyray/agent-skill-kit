# Prep, Stories, and Delivery

Use this reference for `stories`, `prep`, `present`, `practice`, `mock`, and `hype`.

## Storybank

Support four actions: build, add, improve, and audit.

### Build or add

Ask one discovery question at a time. Explore moments involving conflict, ambiguity, influence, failure, speed, tradeoffs, customer insight, technical depth, leadership, learning, and measurable change. Capture what the candidate personally decided and did.

For each story:

1. preserve raw facts before polishing;
2. separate team outcome from candidate contribution;
3. identify missing evidence and metrics;
4. extract the earned secret only if the experience supports one;
5. tag competencies and likely question types;
6. create short and full versions without changing facts.

### Improve or audit

Check relevance, action depth, result evidence, credibility, differentiation, and retrieval speed. Rank gaps as Critical, Addressable, or Covered for the target role. Track reuse so one strong story is not forced into every answer.

### Map stories to a role

Score each candidate story as Strong fit, Workable, Stretch, or Gap for each likely question. Resolve collisions across the whole interview portfolio:

1. reserve strongest-fit stories for the highest-priority signals;
2. prefer variety when quality is comparable;
3. avoid recently overused stories when an equally strong option exists;
4. protect the best earned-secret match;
5. expose uncovered competencies rather than forcing a weak mapping.

## Prep brief

Gather the job description, company, role and level, round, format, date, available interviewer evidence, and storybank. Use current research only when available and necessary.

Build:

- `Interview Format and Coaching Boundary`
- `Verified Company and Role Signals`
- `What This Round Likely Optimizes For`
- `Best Positioning`
- `Role-Fit Assessment`
- `Likely Concerns and Honest Counters`
- `Predicted Questions`
- `Story Mapping and Gaps`
- `Questions to Ask`
- `Day-Of Cheat Sheet`
- `Confidence and Unknowns`

When no resume, profile, or storybank exists, label role fit `Not assessed` and story mapping `Unavailable`; do not fill the gap with generic candidate claims. Do not claim an interview format from generic company lore. Ask the recruiter or candidate when unknown. For technical formats, state that the coach can evaluate communication, problem framing, assumptions, tradeoffs, and clarity but cannot guarantee domain correctness without appropriate evidence or tools. If a suitable technical evaluator or inspection capability is available and authorized, use it and distinguish its evidence from coaching inference.

### Per-interviewer one-pager

When a named interviewer and usable storybank or loop evidence exist, also offer a delivery-ready one-pager. Save it, when approved, as:

`materials/<company>/<interviewer-slug>-one-pager-YYYY-MM-DD.md`

Use this order:

1. how the interviewer plausibly reads the round, based only on verified evidence;
2. the decision bar for this round;
3. recruiter- or interviewer-endorsed material;
4. at-a-glance question → story → earned-secret table;
5. concise answer spines under every high-likelihood question;
6. questions matched to the interviewer's actual lane;
7. last-minute risks and recovery cue.

Retrieve before drafting. Preserve “involved in,” “led,” “owned,” or other scope language exactly. Make the brief for reasoning and the one-pager for delivery; do not collapse both into one bloated document.

## Presentation rounds

Confirm audience, prompt, time, Q&A allocation, required artifact, evaluation criteria, and whether the presentation is live or asynchronous.

Coach:

- opening and decision-oriented thesis;
- narrative arc and section timing;
- evidence density and what to omit;
- assumptions, alternatives, and tradeoffs;
- audience-specific terminology;
- closing and Q&A transitions;
- likely challenges and concise response strategies.

For take-home or async work, clarify prompt ambiguities, agree on a time box, review the outline before the full draft, and prepare live follow-up questions. Do not do undisclosed take-home work that the user is expected to complete independently; coach, critique, and help within the employer's rules.

## Practice

Choose the drill from the bottleneck:

- `ladder`: answer the same question in 30, 60, and 90 seconds;
- `relevance`: decode the question and name its evaluation signal before answering;
- `evidence`: deepen actions, tradeoffs, and results;
- `credibility`: separate team scope, candidate scope, assumptions, and proof;
- `differentiation`: surface the earned secret without becoming performative;
- `retrieval`: select the right story quickly under a timer;
- `pushback`: handle skeptical follow-ups without defensiveness;
- `interruption`: recover and land the point after interruption;
- `stress`: combine time pressure, ambiguity, and follow-ups;
- `role`: use the discipline-specific guide.

Run one question and one answer at a time. Offer a warmup when the candidate is anxious or unfamiliar with the format. After feedback, specify one adjustment for the next rep. Advance difficulty only after repeated evidence, not a single good attempt.

## Mock interviews

Confirm format, target role, duration, difficulty, whether feedback comes after each answer or at the end, and whether the candidate wants voice or text. Ask only the highest-value missing setup question per turn. For urgent sessions, state safe defaults—realistic difficulty, text, and feedback at the end—and invite correction instead of spending several turns on setup.

Run 4–6 questions by default. Stay in interviewer role during the mock. Ask one question at a time; use realistic follow-ups and do not leak the rubric. For panels, identify speakers and vary their lanes without caricature. For mixed interviews, announce only real format transitions an interviewer would make.

Afterward ask for self-assessment, then return:

- `Overall Impression`
- `Arc Analysis`
- `Per-Unit Evidence and Scorecard`
- `Holistic Patterns`
- `Interviewer Read`
- `Top Three Changes`
- `Redo Recommendation`

Use a redo only for the highest-leverage answer or transition. Record mock scores separately from real interview scores.

## Hype and day-of support

Use confirmed strengths, effective stories, and current loop evidence to create confidence grounded in facts. If no state exists, ask for one achievement they trust and the interview format.

Return:

- a 60-second evidence-based hype reel;
- three likely concerns with honest counters;
- three questions to ask;
- one focus cue;
- a 10-minute warmup;
- recovery language for a blank, weak answer, or interruption.

Avoid guarantees, false positivity, breathwork presented as treatment, or introducing major new frameworks immediately before the interview.

# Coaching System

Use this reference for scoring, feedback, challenge, drills, candidate-language drafting, and next-step selection.

## Five-dimension rubric

Score each dimension from 1 to 5 only when enough direct evidence exists.

| Dimension | 1–2 | 3 | 4–5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Substance | assertions, vague actions, missing result | relevant example with some concrete detail | specific decisions, tradeoffs, contribution, and verified result |
| Structure | hard to follow or no answer | understandable but loose or overlong | clear arc, proportionate context, decisive landing |
| Relevance | misses the question or signal | answers broadly with some drift | directly addresses the evaluation signal and level |
| Credibility | inflated, inconsistent, or unsupported | plausible with minor ambiguity | precise scope, honest limits, and corroborating detail |
| Differentiation | generic advice or interchangeable story | competent but familiar | candidate-specific insight, judgment, or earned secret |

For every material score, cite a short observed behavior or excerpt and state confidence. Use `Not scored` when the evidence is memory-only, incomplete, or outside the coach's competence.

## Seniority calibration

Evaluate at the target level, not against a generic ideal.

- **Early career**: clear contribution, learning, basic prioritization, and honest collaboration.
- **Mid-level**: independent ownership, tradeoffs, cross-functional execution, and measurable effect.
- **Senior**: ambiguous problem framing, leverage, durable systems, stakeholder alignment, and second-order effects.
- **Staff, principal, head, or executive**: organization-level judgment, portfolio choices, influence without control, talent or operating-system effects, and explicit opportunity cost.

Do not punish a candidate for lacking executive scope when the target role does not require it. Do not inflate isolated project work into organizational leadership.

## Triage before prescription

Choose the smallest intervention that addresses the dominant bottleneck:

1. **No evidence** → extract or build story material.
2. **Weak question fit** → decode questions and practice relevance.
3. **Good evidence, poor structure** → constraint ladder and answer spine.
4. **Plausibility or scope concerns** → credibility audit and evidence repair.
5. **Competent but generic** → earned-secret and differentiation work.
6. **Delivery collapses under pressure** → retrieval, interruption, or stress drills.
7. **Scores look healthy but outcomes lag** → investigate role fit, targeting, unmeasured factors, and calibration rather than drilling blindly.

Prioritize one root cause even when it affects several dimensions. Do not prescribe five unrelated drills for one underlying problem.

## Evidence and confidence

Separate:

- **Observed**: visible in the supplied artifact or interaction.
- **Reported**: stated by the candidate.
- **Sourced**: supported by a current external source.
- **Inferred**: a coach hypothesis that still needs testing.

Use High confidence for repeated direct evidence, Medium for one clear signal or consistent reported evidence, and Low for incomplete or indirect evidence. Do not launder Low-confidence inference into state truth.

## Differentiation and earned secrets

An earned secret is a non-obvious lesson the candidate can credibly claim because of specific experience. It is not a slogan, generic principle, or contrarian flourish.

Extract it by asking:

1. What did the candidate initially believe?
2. What concrete event or repeated evidence changed that belief?
3. What do they now do differently?
4. What cost, tradeoff, or limitation makes the insight credible?

Use earned secrets to sharpen answers, positioning, resumes, outreach, and questions. Never invent one on the candidate's behalf.

## Coaching voice and directness

- **Level 1**: gentle, affirming, and highly scaffolded.
- **Level 2**: supportive with clear priorities.
- **Level 3**: direct and balanced; default when unspecified.
- **Level 4**: blunt about patterns and tradeoffs.
- **Level 5**: lead with the most important evidence-backed truth and challenge avoidance, but never manufacture a correction to appear rigorous.

At levels 1–4, ask the candidate what they noticed before giving critique. At level 5, state the assessment first, then ask what they see. Directness never permits ridicule, invented certainty, or domain bluffing.

Treat resistance as data, not disobedience. Once the candidate rejects a framing, retire it for the session and record the correction. Challenge the candidate again only with new evidence.

## Candidate answer voice

Draft words the candidate could genuinely say:

- Tell the story rather than selling a polished persona.
- Preserve exact scope language. “Contributed to” does not become “owned.”
- Lead with concrete action and consequence, not self-praise.
- Do not use self-diminishing setup to neutralize a concern.
- Avoid unearned superlatives, truth-prefaces such as “honestly,” and naming the fear being countered.
- Bold or otherwise mark the one earned insight that must land in prep materials, but keep spoken delivery natural.
- Match the candidate's vocabulary and sentence length when examples exist. Offer alternatives rather than overwriting their voice.

## Story and answer quality

Use STAR as a diagnostic container, not a script:

- Situation: only the context needed to understand stakes.
- Task: the candidate's actual responsibility and constraint.
- Action: decisions, alternatives, influence, execution, and adaptation.
- Result: measurable or observable consequence, plus learning.

For “tell me about yourself,” use a present → relevant past → future-fit arc. For strategy or technical answers, use clarify → approach → tradeoffs → decision → validation. For a gap or failure, use fact → ownership → repair → evidence of change.

## Practice protocol

1. Define interview format, role, difficulty, and coaching cadence.
2. Ask one realistic question without pre-coaching the answer.
3. Wait for the full answer. In voice-capable hosts, accept audio only when the user chooses it.
4. Ask for self-assessment unless directness is level 5.
5. Give evidence-backed feedback and, when warranted, scores.
6. Name one adjustment for the next rep.
7. Repeat with a new question, pushback, constraint, or interruption.

Do not reveal an entire mock question set before the mock. Do not repeatedly score the same answer without a meaningful revision.

## Calibration loop

After at least three evidence-backed interviews with outcomes:

1. Compare per-dimension scores with advancement, rejection, and external feedback.
2. Check whether a recurring outcome contradicts coach scoring.
3. Investigate unmeasured factors such as role fit, interview format, target level, communication channel, or process variance.
4. Propose a calibration adjustment and label it provisional until tested.
5. Update `Current Truth` only when the candidate confirms or repeated evidence supports the change.

Learn from successful outcomes as aggressively as failures. Record which stories, signals, and formats correlate with advancement without claiming causation from a small sample.

## Psychological and cultural safeguards

- Normalize ordinary interview anxiety without medicalizing it.
- Offer grounding, rehearsal, pacing, and recovery techniques; do not provide mental-health treatment.
- Distinguish communication norms from competence. Do not penalize accent, dialect, non-native phrasing, disability, age, race, gender, religion, or other protected characteristics.
- Coach clarity and audience adaptation without erasing identity.
- Avoid illegal or jurisdiction-specific employment advice. For legal questions, recommend qualified local guidance.

## Prescriptive next step

End a substantive workflow with:

`**Recommended next**: [mode] - [state-aware reason]. **Alternatives**: [mode], [mode].`

Choose from current time pressure, the primary bottleneck, unprocessed evidence, storybank gaps, and live interview loops. Do not mechanically recommend the next item in a fixed menu.

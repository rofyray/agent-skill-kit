# Compensation

Use this reference for `salary` and `negotiate`. Compensation, employment, tax, equity, and contract information can be time-sensitive and jurisdiction-specific. Research current facts when available, cite sources, and distinguish coaching from legal, tax, or financial advice.

## Safety and privacy

- Ask which compensation details may be stored. Default to a strategic summary rather than complete offer documents.
- Do not upload, forward, accept, reject, sign, or send an offer or negotiation message without explicit authorization.
- Do not encourage false competing offers, fabricated market data, or concealed material facts.
- Flag terms that need qualified legal, tax, immigration, or financial review instead of interpreting them conclusively.

## Salary conversations before an offer

Establish jurisdiction, role and level, location, stage, available range, candidate target, current-comp disclosure constraints, and leverage. When the candidate lacks a target, help them build a range from current, credible sources; do not invent market benchmarks.

Separate:

- base salary;
- cash bonus or commission and its conditions;
- equity type, amount, vesting, exercise or tax considerations;
- sign-on, relocation, benefits, time off, flexibility, title, scope, and review timing.

Coach the candidate to delay unnecessary anchoring while remaining direct. A useful early-stage response often:

1. expresses interest;
2. asks for the approved range or total-comp structure;
3. situates expectations in role, level, location, and total package;
4. avoids volunteering current compensation unless the candidate chooses and local rules allow it.

Return a primary script, pushback response, research gaps, and stage-specific next move. Clearly mark placeholders.

## Analyze an offer

Extract every material component and label unknown or contingent values. Normalize recurring and one-time components separately; do not present speculative equity value as cash.

Check:

- level, title, scope, manager, location, and start date;
- base, target and guaranteed variable pay;
- equity instrument, units or value basis, vesting, cliff, refresh policy, exercise window, and liquidity assumptions;
- sign-on and repayment clauses;
- benefits and time off;
- severance, non-compete or restrictive terms, IP, arbitration, and contingencies for qualified review;
- response deadline and approval chain.

For multiple offers, compare like with like and include role quality, growth, risk, and candidate preferences - not just a single total-comp number.

## Build negotiation strategy

Use a GAINS sequence:

1. **Goals**: identify must-haves, preferences, and walk-away conditions.
2. **Alternatives**: clarify real options and timing without bluffing.
3. **Information**: list known package facts, gaps, and decision-makers.
4. **Needs**: connect each ask to role scope, market evidence, transition cost, or candidate value.
5. **Sequence**: prioritize asks, choose channel, script the opening, anticipate responses, and define next steps.

Create an ask ladder:

- primary ask and evidence;
- acceptable movement;
- alternative levers if the primary is fixed;
- decision condition and deadline.

Do not overload the first conversation with every possible ask. Preserve room to listen and learn.

## Practice a compensation call

Simulate one exchange at a time. Score:

- information gathering;
- clarity of ask;
- evidence and credibility;
- composure and silence;
- listening and adaptation;
- next-step control.

Do not score success by whether the employer immediately agrees. Coach the candidate to pause, summarize, and request written confirmation.

## Return

Use:

- `Situation and Unknowns`
- `Offer or Range Analysis`
- `Leverage and Evidence`
- `Prioritized Ask Ladder`
- `Primary Script`
- `Likely Responses and Replies`
- `Legal, Tax, or Financial Review Flags`
- `Timeline and Next Step`

Keep candidate-ready language separate from private coaching analysis.

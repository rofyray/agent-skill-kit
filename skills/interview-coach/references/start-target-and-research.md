# Start, Target, and Research

Use this reference for `kickoff`, `status`, `research`, `decode`, `concerns`, and `questions`.

## Kickoff

Set up only the context that will change coaching. Ask one question at a time.

1. Ask what the candidate needs most and whether an interview is already scheduled.
2. Establish target role, level, domain, location constraints, and timeline.
3. Ask for the resume or a concise experience summary. Do not require both.
4. Ask which interview formats or stages they expect and what has felt hardest.
5. Ask for feedback directness from 1–5; default to 3.
6. If durable state is desired, obtain location and storage consent, then initialize it.
7. Extract a provisional profile, strengths, risks, transition context, and the highest-leverage next action.

Do not interrogate the candidate for every possible field. Mark unknowns and learn them from later interactions. Revisit the global profile whenever new stories, transcripts, job descriptions, or feedback reveal durable candidate-wide evidence.

Return:

- `Kickoff Summary`
- `Profile Snapshot`
- `Readiness and Time Mode` - urgent (0–2 days), focused (3–14 days), or developmental (15+ days/no date)
- `Primary Coaching Hypothesis`
- `First Plan` - now, before the next event, and later

## Status

For `status`, load core state and only the siblings needed to answer. Report:

- the nearest event or unresolved outcome;
- current primary bottleneck and confidence;
- live loops and missing materials;
- highest-leverage next action.

## Research a company

Use `research` to decide whether or how to pursue a company; use `prep` when an interview is active.

Choose depth from the request and time:

- **Quick scan**: official company/product pages, role page, and one reliable current overview.
- **Standard**: add leadership, recent material developments, product/customer signals, and credible employee or interview-process evidence.
- **Deep dive**: add competitors, strategy tensions, leadership interviews, product reviews, and role-relevant operating questions.

Research sequence:

1. Confirm exact company, role, region, and decision.
2. Prefer first-party and primary sources; capture publication or retrieval dates.
3. Separate verified facts from candidate reports, general patterns, and inference.
4. Evaluate fit against the candidate's actual targets and constraints.
5. Identify what remains unknown and how to verify it with a recruiter or interviewer.

Return:

- `Company Snapshot`
- `Role-Relevant Signals`
- `Culture and Operating Evidence`
- `Fit Assessment`
- `Risks and Unknowns`
- `If You Pursue This`
- source links close to supported claims

Never treat anonymous interview reports as definitive process truth. Never infer an individual's preferences from a title alone.

## Decode a job description

Accept pasted text, a file, or a direct posting URL. For URLs:

1. Use an available browser, fetch, or approved read-only retrieval capability.
2. Follow ordinary redirects only to the direct posting.
3. If the page is incomplete, inspect accessible structured metadata when the host supports it.
4. If access is blocked, login-walled, or dependent on unsupported rendering, ask for pasted text or an exported file.

Reject search-result pages as insufficient evidence. Do not bypass authentication or access controls.

Decode with six lenses:

1. repeated competencies;
2. order and emphasis;
3. required versus preferred qualifications;
4. verbs that signal expected scope;
5. reasonable between-the-lines implications;
6. important omissions and questions they create.

Then evaluate role fit across requirement coverage, seniority alignment, domain relevance, competency overlap, and trajectory coherence. Use Strong fit, Investable stretch, or Long shot; attach confidence based on available profile and role evidence.

For 2–5 roles, offer batch triage. Compare common competencies, divergence, fit, candidate enthusiasm, and effort allocation. Do not reduce the decision to keyword overlap.

Return:

- `Decode Summary`
- `Competency Map`
- `Six-Lens Read`
- `Fit Assessment`
- `Likely Evaluation Signals`
- `Verify With Recruiter`
- `Learn to Read This Yourself`

## Anticipate concerns

Infer concerns only from the target bar and candidate evidence. Rank them:

- **Potential dealbreakers**: missing legal eligibility, required credential, or essential scope.
- **Significant**: plausible experience, level, domain, or narrative risk.
- **Minor**: presentational friction that can be clarified quickly.

For each concern include evidence, confidence, an honest counter, proof to surface, and a verification question. Do not script concealment or misrepresentation. Route evidence gaps to stories, resume, or recruiter clarification.

## Generate questions to ask

Tailor questions to stage and interviewer's verified lane:

- recruiter: process, scope, level, constraints, compensation timing;
- hiring manager: outcomes, priorities, team interfaces, decision rights;
- peer or cross-functional partner: collaboration, tradeoffs, working norms;
- domain expert: technical or craft decisions and learning boundaries;
- executive: strategy, organizational constraints, success horizon.

Use company research and loop evidence only when it changes the question. Avoid questions answered clearly by supplied materials, performative questions, premature benefits questions, and prompts outside the interviewer's likely scope.

Return five prioritized questions, why each matters, and the best two if time is short.

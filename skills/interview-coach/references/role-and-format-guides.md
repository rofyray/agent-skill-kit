# Role and Format Guides

Use this reference to tailor prep, practice, mocks, and analysis. These are coaching lenses, not substitutes for employer-specific evidence.

## Role lenses

### Product management

Probe problem selection, customer evidence, prioritization, metrics, tradeoffs, execution with engineering and design, decision reversibility, and strategy-to-delivery connection. Ask how the candidate handled conflicting signals and what changed because of their judgment.

### Software engineering

Probe requirements clarification, architecture boundaries, tradeoffs, failure modes, performance, security, observability, debugging, testing, and collaboration. Coach the candidate to expose assumptions and reason aloud. Do not claim the design or code is technically correct without appropriate inspection and expertise.

### Design

Probe research foundation, problem framing, alternatives, rationale, constraints, accessibility, iteration, stakeholder influence, and product impact. Distinguish polished visuals from design judgment.

### Data science and analytics

Probe problem definition, data quality, leakage and bias, method selection, baselines, evaluation, uncertainty, deployment, monitoring, and stakeholder decisions. Separate model metrics from business outcomes.

### Research

Probe question quality, study design, sampling, ethics, analysis, triangulation, insight strength, limitations, and influence on decisions. Do not reward certainty that exceeds the evidence.

### Operations and business operations

Probe diagnosis, process mapping, controls, cross-functional ownership, implementation, measurement, change management, and sustainability. Ask what would break at scale.

### Marketing and growth

Probe audience and insight, positioning, channel strategy, creative judgment, experimentation, attribution limits, budget tradeoffs, incrementality, and learning loops. Separate correlation from causation.

### Leadership and executive roles

Probe portfolio choices, operating cadence, organization design, resource allocation, stakeholder governance, talent decisions, durable systems, and opportunity cost. Require specificity without forcing confidential information.

If a role is not listed, infer a small evaluation lens from the job description and verified interview format. State the inference.

## Interview formats

### Recruiter screen

Optimize for coherent target, relevant scope, motivation, logistics, level, communication, and compensation handling. Keep answers concise and preserve questions for the recruiter's lane.

### Behavioral

Parse interviewer question → evaluation signal → story choice → evidence → learning. Watch for context bloat, vague collective pronouns, missing decisions, result-only claims, and moralized hindsight.

### Hiring-manager or domain interview

Balance relevant depth with business context. Expect follow-ups about tradeoffs, disagreements, scope, and what the candidate would change.

### Panel

Track speaker, lane, question, answer, interruption, and cross-interviewer signal. Coach eye-line or verbal inclusion where relevant, recovery after interruption, and consistency across repeated questions.

### System design

Evaluate communication phases: clarify, define constraints, propose approach, explore components, surface tradeoffs and failure modes, adapt to prompts, and summarize. Add domain-correctness conclusions only when supported by appropriate tools or expertise.

### Case study

Evaluate problem framing, assumptions, structure, analysis, options, recommendation, risk, and adaptation. Distinguish candidate-driven case presentations from interviewer-led cases.

### Technical plus behavioral

Track mode switches explicitly. Evaluate whether the candidate can move from implementation detail to ownership and collaboration without losing coherence.

### Presentation or portfolio review

Evaluate narrative arc, content density, audience calibration, evidence, timing, transitions, and Q&A adaptability. Coach the work and delivery separately.

### Compensation or offer call

Treat as a distinct negotiation interaction. Score listening, information gathering, composure, clarity of ask, evidence, tradeoffs, and next-step control - not the dollar outcome alone.

## Interviewer perspectives

Use perspectives to create realistic follow-ups without stereotyping:

- friendly ally: warm tone, still tests evidence;
- skeptical specialist: probes assumptions and credibility;
- cross-functional partner: tests collaboration and tradeoffs;
- time-pressed leader: interrupts and demands the decision;
- people leader: tests motivation, growth, judgment, and working style.

Base named-interviewer tailoring on verified profile, role, public work, or recruiter guidance. A title alone supports only a low-confidence lane hypothesis.

## Difficulty scaling

- **Foundational**: direct question, one follow-up, generous thinking time.
- **Realistic**: ambiguous prompt, two follow-ups, normal time pressure.
- **Advanced**: competing constraints, interruption, contradiction, or evidence challenge.
- **Stress**: combine several pressures only with candidate consent; stop if it stops being useful.

Increase difficulty when the candidate repeatedly meets the current bar. Never use pressure as theater or confuse discomfort with learning.

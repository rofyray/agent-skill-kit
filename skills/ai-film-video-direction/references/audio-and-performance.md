# Audio and Performance

Use this reference to integrate playable character behavior and an intentional soundtrack into a video shot without replacing a dedicated performance bible or audio workflow.

## Build a minimum performance block

When approved performance direction exists, preserve its stable character core, scene objective, tactics, voice identity, physical state, and leaving state.

When it does not exist, resolve only what the shot needs:

- Entering emotional, cognitive, and physical state.
- Immediate objective and obstacle.
- What the character hides, withholds, or misreads.
- Current tactic and the trigger for any change.
- Attention, listening, gaze, breath, posture, distance, and task behavior visible at this shot size.
- Dialogue delivery arising from the tactic and physical state.
- Reaction and state that leave the shot.

Direct actions toward partners or obstacles rather than asking characters to display emotions. A pause needs an internal event such as assessing, deciding, withholding, recovering, or changing tactics.

## Scale behavior to the shot

- In a close view, small changes in attention, breath, facial tension, and timing may carry the beat.
- In a wide view, body orientation, distance, path, group hierarchy, and interaction with the environment must read.
- In a brief shot, prioritize the one trigger and change the edit needs.
- In a longer take, preserve state inertia and let behavior evolve through resistance.
- In an ensemble, assign individual knowledge, objectives, focus, and reaction causes. Avoid mechanically synchronized reactions.

Do not prescribe a fixed number of blinks, gestures, breaths, or expressions unless a precise action is story-critical.

## Assign every spoken line

For dialogue, specify:

- Exact text, language, and speaker.
- Voice identity reference and its role when supplied.
- Delivery, pace, emphasis, breath, effort, distance, and vocal condition.
- Start phase or time, interruption, overlap, and finish when timing matters.
- Whether the speaker is on screen, offscreen, obscured, or silent but reacting.
- Which visible mouths remain silent.
- Lip-sync requirement and acceptable tolerance.

Never put a character's line in another character's description. If the locked text cannot fit intelligibly within the duration, report the conflict instead of silently shortening or accelerating it.

## Design the soundtrack by layers

Use only the layers the scene requires:

- Dialogue.
- Nonverbal voice, breath, exertion, laughter, crying, or effort.
- Synchronous action effects and impacts.
- Prop, clothing, footsteps, machinery, vehicle, and environmental effects.
- Room tone or location ambience.
- Music, source music, or score when intended.

Do not default every shot to sound effects only. Music may be absent, embedded in the generated clip, produced separately, or reserved for editorial. State the intended workflow.

Continuous ambience can glue edits, but it must match location, time, weather, perspective, and camera distance. A sound event should have an owner, source, timing, and spatial relation when those affect meaning.

## Match audio to physical events

Sound should respect cause, travel, contact, scale, environment, and perspective. A heavy impact, distant event, enclosed room, masked face, underwater movement, or moving camera changes what is heard and when.

Use audio to reinforce thresholds and state changes, such as a latch engaging, pressure release, cloth tension, engine load, room tone dropping, or a voice losing support. Avoid sound that announces an event before its intended trigger unless anticipation is deliberate.

## Adapt to tool capability

If the video model supports reliable audio, map dialogue, speaker roles, voice references, ambience, music, and effects into its actual fields.

If it lacks audio or cannot control multiple speakers, generate silent picture and return a separate audio handoff containing:

- Locked dialogue and time map.
- Voice identities and delivery notes.
- Lip-sync or mouth-closure requirements.
- Effects cue list.
- Ambience and music plan.
- Sync landmarks and edit handles.

Do not claim that audio, voice, or lip sync exists unless the returned clip has been inspected or the tool reports verifiable completion.

## Verify performance and audio

Inspect:

- Objectives, attention, listening, triggers, tactic changes, and state carryover.
- Character-specific behavior instead of generalized animation.
- Speaker ownership, exact wording, language, timing, overlap, and silent mouths.
- Voice identity, vocal state, intelligibility, and lip sync.
- Physical effort and sound synchronized to action.
- Effects, ambience, and music continuity across cuts.
- Unwanted extra speech, duplicate voices, synthetic artifacts, or abrupt audio edges.

When only one layer fails, repair or replace that layer without discarding an accepted picture or performance unless the tool makes them inseparable.

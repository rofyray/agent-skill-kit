# Hyper-Motion Profile

Direct a product through one coherent mechanic. Energy is configurable: this profile includes aggressive assembly and camera travel, restrained material studies, and precise mechanical decompositions. Treat speed as a creative choice. Do not make every product fly through a tunnel.

## Choose the mechanic

| Variant | Specify | Protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assembly and transformation | Ordered parts, trajectories, contacts, compression, final silhouette | Every landed part persists; transformations have explicit physical exceptions. |
| Environmental product study | Product reference, material detail, recurring props, light, depth, one exceptional action | Label and silhouette fidelity, consistent scale, restrained prop occlusion. |
| Exploded mechanism | Verified component inventory, separation axes, spacing, macro path, reverse assembly | Rigid parts, plausible order, exact technical claims and anchored callouts. |

Begin with a readable action or an intentional reveal. Organize the film into a cause, a heightened visual development, a return or resolution, and a hero state when appropriate. Carry transitions with inherited motion direction, matching silhouette, occlusion, or a designed cut. State which sequence is one take and where cuts are allowed.

## Product and material contract

Lock shape, proportions, label, material, colors, component count, and diagnostic features to the product reference. State how long foreground objects may occlude the identity when it matters; choose the limit from the actual frame rate and reading need. Maintain a fixed recurring prop cast. Use alternate canonical views for macro details; do not invent unseen mechanisms for a real product presented as technically accurate.

For assembly, write a state ledger: part absent, traveling, contacting, settled, and retained. Specify how the visible silhouette demonstrates earlier layers after later ones arrive. A contact may compress a soft layer or displace liquid, but it must not erase it. For reverse assembly, preserve the same parts and axes; verify that the final component count agrees with the opening.

Describe material response concretely: solid slices bend but retain their identity; metal parts stay rigid; droplets merge and fall under gravity; soft packaging compresses and rebounds; glossy surfaces retain reflections across movement. Distinguish realistic world physics from an intentional suspension or abstract void. Bound any exception in time and restore the normal rules afterward. Avoid incompatible demands such as a stable artificial orbit described as unforced terrestrial gravity.

## Camera and rhythm

For energetic work, define acceleration, path, near passes, direction, parallax, deceleration, and sharp landings. Give the viewer a clear anchor during speed ramps. A counter-orbit requires camera and parts moving in opposite specified directions. For restrained work, use continuous drifts, focus changes, controlled levitation, and a small number of impacts; do not inherit aggressive moves from the profile name.

Separate camera speed from playback rate. A macro hold must provide enough time to read a callout and inspect the part. Technical lockups should have one dominant value, a subordinate label, and a leader pinned to a real anchor. If the text must stay upright for reading, define the billboard behavior independently of the world anchor.

Use the approved final image as an endpoint only when supported. Otherwise build the end card deterministically, match its arrival to the motion, and hold it for the specified duration. Scope "continuous motion" to the action passage so it does not contradict a final still hold.

## Original prompt pattern

An original travel speaker separates along its actual housing axis into shell, grille, and approved internal module. A camera pass reveals the module, one verified specification appears beside a pinned leader, and the same components return in reverse order. Sound can punctuate seating contacts. If reliable internal references are absent, replace the decomposition with an exterior material and control study.

## Acceptance and fallback

Check product identity at every landing, component persistence, assembly order, contact response, label readability, reflection and exposure continuity, anchored type, and the final frame. Reduce crossing particles, combine secondary components, shorten the camera path, or render natural segments if the mechanic overwhelms the model. Protect the central mechanic while simplifying. A deterministic end card is preferable to spending repeated takes trying to repair a correct product shot's lettering.

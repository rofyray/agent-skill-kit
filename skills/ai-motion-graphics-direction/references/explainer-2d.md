# 2D Explainer Profile

Choose `pure-vector` or `hybrid-2d-3d` from the brief. Begin with the concept or process the viewer must understand, then make each beat demonstrate one causal step. Flat imagery alone does not make an explainer clear. Use a recurring character or object only when it gives the explanation useful continuity.

## Pure-vector variant

Define shape vocabulary, stroke or no-stroke rules, fill roles, corners, proportions, facial features, typography, ground contact, and a fixed prop set. Lock the recurring character's body and clothing across poses. Restrict or permit textures, gradients, and shadows deliberately; flat does not automatically mean a universal ban on every surface treatment.

Treat the frame as a graphic stage when requested. Motion comes from translation, scaling, shape transitions, stagger, anticipation, stretch, settle, and held poses. An enlarged phone or diagram is an element scale change, not a dolly. Use one leading shape per transition so the eye can follow a bag becoming a mat or a circle becoming a pin. Specify entry and exit geometry for any morph; lettering should retain complete forms during its reading window.

Choose squash and stretch by object family; rigid UI does not inherit a character's elastic body. Smear drawings may clarify a fast action while optical blur would soften the entire design. Keep enough still time after a major movement to read and understand. Rapid full-frame color switches are optional, and large flashes or repeated strobing should be redesigned when they impede comfortable viewing.

Use simplified functional UI if no exact interface is required. When an actual product flow matters, preserve approved labels and order in deterministic layers. Avoid made-up prices, offers, availability, or button behavior.

## Hybrid-2D-3D variant

Write a layer contract before the beats:

| Layer | Owns | Does not inherit by accident |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3D | Perspective, light, material response, contact, reflection, volume | Flat vector fills merely because a character touches it. |
| 2D | Flat shapes, graphic color, illustrated poses and timing | Surface shading, cast shadows, or volume from surrounding 3D. |
| Composite | Masking, contact alignment, depth order, designed reflections | Unplanned style conversion between layers. |

Allow deliberate exceptions, such as a flat character appearing as a mirror image in glass, without shading the character itself. Describe the physical interaction: a character's landing nudges a rendered platform, a flat hand aligns to a solid handle, or a cloth ribbon settles around the illustrated body. Inspect contacts and masks rather than relying on a generic "hybrid" label.

Maintain a shared color target across the layers while accounting for how lighting changes the 3D surfaces. Define the common light rig for 3D objects, then keep vector fills controlled separately. Coordinate cinematic travel and graphic action so they do not compete for the same moment of emphasis.

## Type and ending

Specify actual headline strings, hierarchy, emphasis, entrance, hold, and exit. Avoid prescribing an accent word in every sentence when it weakens hierarchy. A word highlight should sit behind intact letterforms and arrive at an intentional time. Choose a conclusion that completes the explanation; a CTA is optional.

## Original prompt pattern

Explain a refill service through one reusable flat bottle: it empties, travels to a refill point, and returns full while the same object identity remains visible. Use a three-state indicator with exact labels in a deterministic layer. For a hybrid version, make the bottle a rendered object and keep the instructional arrows and character flat; specify where their paths pass in front of or behind the bottle.

## Acceptance and fallback

Verify concept order, character proportions, object counts, shape continuity, exact copy, usable dwell time, and stable graphic edges. In hybrid work, check layer properties and every contact. If the generator drifts into shaded animation, render the vector layers deterministically. If a morph becomes illegible, use a matching shape cut with clear before and after states. A video that looks vector-based is still raster footage unless editable vectors were actually created; label deliverables accordingly.

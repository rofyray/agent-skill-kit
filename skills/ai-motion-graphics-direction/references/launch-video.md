# Launch Video Profile

Use for a product, feature, service, or brand reveal. Start from a supplied brief, a website that can actually be inspected, or an authorized reference. Extract the product's demonstrated function, approved benefit, audience, distinguishing feature, brand system, and desired ending. Keep research about the product within the creative brief; do not turn the work into customer acquisition or offer strategy.

## Two entry paths

From a brief or URL, turn one meaningful change into a visual mechanic: a blocked task becomes a completed action, scattered inputs organize into a result, or a concealed feature becomes visible through use. Define the problem, interaction, outcome, and identity moment. A film can contain several shots while retaining one design world.

From a reference, inspect beat boundaries, dominant forms, type scale, arrival and exit curves, transitions, camera behavior, audio cues, and the state at each landing. Extract that motion grammar into an original treatment for the current brand. Match a supplied layout or asset precisely only within the authorized scope. If the reference video cannot be inspected, distinguish user-described behavior from observed behavior.

## Variants

- Spatial product or UI film: establish foreground, middle, and background layers. Camera movement must reveal thickness, parallax, and changing occlusion when real depth is intended. Keep product geometry rigid unless the mechanic requires deformation. Use macro inserts to clarify an event, not to add unrelated texture shots.
- Flat kinetic type: lock the frame and animate layers. Define word or letter build, color accent, card and chip choreography, collapse or expansion, shape-match transitions, and the final reading window. Layer scaling is not camera travel.
- Reference-led adaptation: preserve the useful rhythm while rebuilding copy, brand assets, visual metaphors, and layout for the new message and aspect ratio.

Use state changes to prove the function. Two conversational symbols should respond in turns; an analysis bar should not change value because its highlight moves; a recording scan should lead to a summary that follows logically. Separate product action from the overlay headline so text remains clear during travel. Keep decorative labels out of blank regions reserved for an editor or platform UI.

## Production recipe

1. Write the one-sentence mechanic and exact message sequence.
2. Lock the palette, materials or flat shape system, product identity, typography, and transition grammar.
3. Create a storyboard when the visual world is unproven. Nine frames is a useful option for a short multi-beat concept, not a required shot count. Label times and copy; fix composition at this stage.
4. Map shots or beats to interaction states. Define entry, travel, landing, reading time, and exit.
5. Compile separate plate and graphics instructions where exact type or UI is required.
6. Resolve the ending: brand, benefit, and action only when the brief calls for them. Explicitly hold, loop, or fade; prohibit an appended scene only when that failure matters.

## Original prompt pattern

For a fictional scheduling product, use a mechanic in which loose appointment tiles dock into one weekly grid. Begin with one conflicting tile, show a controlled reassignment, and finish on the resolved schedule. Give each tile a fixed identity, keep times and labels in a deterministic overlay, and let one traveling accent indicate the active tile. The story is resolved scheduling, so avoid generic floating dashboards that never demonstrate the change.

Adapt this pattern using the shared prompt compiler; do not adopt its subject or style by default.

## Acceptance and fallback

Verify that the function can be understood without reading every label; exact copy is stable; the benefit is supported; layers do not obscure important type; highlights do not alter data; the product retains its form; and the ending matches the brief. If the model produces a sequence of zoomed stills, reduce the scene to one spatial relationship and prove it with a short parallax test. If lettering fails, retain the approved plate and composite the copy. If action density prevents reading, reduce copy or beat count before extending generation blindly.

# Editorial Explainer Profile

Start with the explanation and evidence. Build a compact claim ledger: claim ID, approved wording, source, date or period, units, denominator, uncertainty, and which scene proves it. Separate measured facts, explanatory schematics, hypothetical examples, and visual metaphors. If factual verification is unavailable, retain placeholders or qualitative claims and state the limitation before presenting finished factual media.

The inspiration article contains illustrative numerical overlays in its voice example. Do not promote those numbers, equal-share diagrams, or technical labels into verified scientific facts merely because their graphic geometry is precise. An exact-looking chart can still make a false claim. Verify the current subject independently from appropriate primary sources.

## Narrative and visual system

Build a question, a causal mechanism, a consequence, and a resolution when that structure fits. Keep the same person, object, or system through the explanation rather than swapping unrelated cutouts. Each transition should carry meaning or an identifying shape: a button can match an arrow, a target can match a speaker, or a timeline marker can lead into a chart.

Choose one editorial system: paper collage, restrained diagrams, magazine typography, archival montage, data graphics, or a designed combination. For paper work, define texture, print dots, edge treatment, shadow direction, and matte surfaces. Oversized crops and bold color blocks can create energy without continuous camera shake. For other looks, replace those primitives intentionally instead of retaining paper effects by default.

Use one dominant composition color and one strongest focus per beat where it improves comprehension. Resolve each composition quickly, allow a reading window, then move to the next idea. Use sequential activation to demonstrate causes; simultaneous oscillation or movement can imply relationships the evidence does not support.

## Data layer

Create charts and labels from a single approved data source in deterministic graphics when possible. Store the source and unit with the value. A percentage needs an explicit denominator and full track; a comparison needs consistent scales or an obvious explanation of different scales. Calculate geometry from data, not from descriptive guesses. Distinguish a conceptual pathway from a measured share; use qualitative labels when the source does not establish percentages.

For a percentage shown as final throughout a beat, draw the fill at its final length from its first visible frame. If animation is requested, update the displayed value with the fill from the same interpolation, or reveal the final label only after the bar settles. Do not show a fixed 50% beside a temporarily full bar. Printed numerals should appear complete and remain stable rather than morphing through wrong digits.

For split or stacked bars, verify the components, total, bounds, and labels. For changes over time, label period, baseline, and whether a change is relative percent or percentage points. Avoid implying magnitude with unjustified icon counts, areas, thicknesses, color saturation, or sound intensity. A variable-width waveform may be metaphorical; it is not automatically a quantitative acoustic measurement.

Integrate analytics into actual negative space. A chart must not cover the causal action or force a second reading task during a rapid reveal. A dense overlay may require a dedicated beat. Put readable source notes on the frame or in an accompanying source card as appropriate to the delivery format.

## Narration and timing

Approve exact narration, then align visuals to its semantic sequence. Give each claim enough time to be spoken and understood; extend duration, simplify the explanation, or divide it into scenes when necessary. Keep music below the voice and reserve effects for relevant actions. A paper character need not lip-sync just because narration exists.

Inspect the final speech for omissions, extra words, incorrect units, pronunciations, and truncated endings. If the video model cannot preserve the script, use a separate narration track and retime graphics to it. Captions, emphasis headlines, chart labels, and source notes have different roles and should not fight for the same region.

## Original prompt pattern

Using an approved internal survey with 80 of 200 responses selecting an option, show the question, reveal the choice, then draw a bar whose fill is 40% of a clearly bounded full track. Display the sample size and source period. Use the same calculated value for the label and fill, and end with the limited conclusion supported by that survey. Do not present the sample as the entire population.

## Acceptance and fallback

Audit facts and visual math separately. Verify each claim against its source, then verify exact copy, chart values, denominators, geometry, scales, legend mappings, reading time, narrative causality, and narration alignment. Inspect every frame of a changing chart when practical, not only its endpoint. If a model fails digits or lines, replace that layer deterministically; if the evidence is weak, change the claim rather than polish the graphic. Preserve the source ledger in the final handoff.
